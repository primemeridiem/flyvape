What is measured, and what is made up
Measured
Every neuron, every synapse and its sign, from electron microscopy. The soma positions you are looking at. How fly smell receptors respond to vanillin, acetaldehyde, propylene glycol, glycerol, menthol and nicotine. Which dopamine neurons reach which mushroom-body outputs.
Made up, on purpose
Nicotine as extra drive on neurons in proportion to their acetylcholine inputs. Death from sustained over-firing while nicotine is bound at a neuron's receptors, the calcium route nicotinic receptors open. The dose rising every 10 puffs as tolerance builds. The e-liquid recipe. Reward dopamine on every puff. Compressed time: each rig window is 24 ms of brain. The fly's body is NeuroMechFly, built from a scan of a real fruit fly, scaled so its eyes sit over the brain's optic lobes and posed by hand. Its wings open on the rig's schedule and beat faster after each dopamine reward, and faster still as rewards add up, slowed down to be visible. The simulated neurons do not drive them. The tether, button pad and vape are drawn. Only the neurons sit at measured positions, drawn enlarged beside the fly with a dashed line to where they sit in its head. The fly pressing AIR, PUFF and WASH shows the rig's schedule. The simulated fly does not choose when to vape.
Worth knowing
A fly's nose barely detects nicotine itself. What it smells is the flavour and the aldehyde from heated propylene glycol. About 23,800 neurons, mostly sensory cells whose bodies sit in the antenna, eye or legs, have no cell body inside the scanned volume. 22,827 of them are drawn at the average position of the cells they connect to; 1,295 connect to nothing placed and are not drawn.
